How to reopen apps after restart in Windows 11

A reboot can interrupt a busy desktop. Windows can relaunch some supported apps, Startup can open a fixed list, and a saved workspace can rebuild the apps and positions you chose. Pick the behavior you actually need.

Short answerLook under Settings → Accounts → Sign-in options for Automatically save my restartable apps and restart them when I sign back in. If your PC offers it, enable it and test the apps you care about; support varies by app. Use Settings → Apps → Startup for apps that should open at every sign-in. To relaunch a chosen app group in saved positions, launch a PowerToys workspace or a suko mode after signing in.

Three different meanings of “restore my apps”

People often want one of three outcomes after restarting Windows. Resume means reopening the apps that were running before the reboot. Startup means opening the same chosen apps at every sign-in. Workspace restoration means opening a particular task setup, including its window layout. They overlap, but none automatically guarantees the same documents, browser tabs and positions.

Save important work before restarting. Relaunching an application is not the same as recovering an unsaved document. That depends on the application's own recovery and session settings.

Try Windows' restartable-apps setting

  1. Open Settings → Accounts → Sign-in options.
  2. If available on your Windows version, turn on Automatically save my restartable apps and restart them when I sign back in.
  3. Open one app you want to test, restart Windows normally, sign back in and check whether it returns.
  4. Repeat with another app. Do not assume that a successful test with one program covers every program on the PC.

Apps need to participate in Windows' restart mechanism; Microsoft documents the Application Restart registration used by developers. The sign-in option is therefore best treated as a convenience for supported apps, not a complete backup of the desktop. Browser tab recovery is also controlled by the browser's own startup or session preferences.

If an app does not return, first check whether the sign-in option is present and enabled, then test whether the app itself offers a “continue where you left off” setting. There is no reliable reason to force every application into Startup simply because one app does not support restart.

Open the same apps at every sign-in

For a password manager, chat app or other tool you need every day, open Settings → Apps → Startup and turn on its entry if Windows lists it. Microsoft covers this method and the Startup folder in its Startup applications guide. Test one app at a time so you can tell which entry is responsible for a slow or crowded sign-in.

Startup does not remember the previous session. It launches the configured apps even if you had closed them before restarting. Keep task-specific editors, games and research tools out of Startup when you do not need them at every sign-in. Rebuild a chosen layout after a reboot

Suppose your Work setup needs an editor, project folder, work browser profile and chat across two monitors. You want that arrangement when you begin Work, but you do not want it to open on every sign-in. Save the group as a workspace and launch it when you are ready.

PowerToys Workspaces

PowerToys Workspaces captures open applications and their positions. Create a workspace, arrange the apps, select Capture, then save a desktop shortcut. After a restart, use the shortcut to open the saved group and move the windows into place. Microsoft notes that launch behavior differs between applications; some reuse an existing window while others open another instance. Test your actual setup before relying on it.

suko modes

A suko mode groups the apps, files, URLs, browser profile, layout and Windows settings for one task. After signing in, switch to your Work mode with its hotkey. It can open the managed app group, restore the saved multi-monitor positions and apply settings such as volume or Do Not Disturb. When you later switch to Gaming or Study, choose which managed Work apps should close.

A mode is a repeatable task setup, not a promise to recover every document that happened to be open before the reboot. The suko mode walkthrough shows the app and layout controls. See our PowerToys Workspaces comparison when deciding whether launch and placement alone cover your routine.

If the desktop still looks wrong

  • Apps reopen but windows move: that is a placement problem. Check the display configuration and use the window-position guide before changing Startup settings.
  • The wrong apps open at sign-in: review Settings → Apps → Startup and each app's own startup preference. A saved workspace should run only when you launch it.
  • A browser opens without the old tabs: review its session-restore setting. Windows reopening the browser process does not guarantee tab recovery.
  • A saved workspace opens duplicates: check how that app handles existing windows. PowerToys documents a Move existing windows option and app-specific launch arguments.

Common questions

Can Windows 11 reopen every app exactly as it was?

No single Windows setting guarantees that. The restartable-apps option depends on app support, and each app controls its own document or tab recovery. A saved workspace can relaunch selected apps and positions.

What is the difference between restartable apps and Startup?

Restartable apps are meant to bring back supported apps from the previous session. Startup launches a fixed configured list at every sign-in, even if those apps were not running before the reboot.

Can I restore my Work setup without launching it every morning?

Yes. Save it as a PowerToys workspace or suko mode and trigger it when you begin Work. Test the apps and layout after a real restart.
